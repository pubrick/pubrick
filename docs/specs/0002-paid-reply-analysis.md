# Paid analysis after automatic Telegram reply collection

**Status:** Proposed design; no implementation or rollout implied.
**Scope:** Source stories and owned Telegram publications only.

## Decision and existing contract

The remaining verified legacy behavior is hourly Gemini analysis of replies to relevant source posts and our own published Telegram posts. The legacy worker fetched replies and analyzed them in one task, wrote a result even for empty or inaccessible discussions, and recorded an estimated spend afterward. Pubrick already has a safer split: separate, default-off hourly **free** collection for source stories and publications; saved text-only samples of at most 50 replies; and a manual, metered **Google BYOK** analysis of the newest 30 saved replies, with at most 500 characters per reply. This increment adds a second, explicit default-off consent to run that existing analysis automatically **only after a successful, nonempty automatic collection**. It does not analyze manual collections, old samples, private discussions, inaccessible posts, or empty/error checks. It does not create topics, drafts, feedback signals, replies, or publications.

Keep the current hourly collection eligibility and fairness limits: public active Telegram source, scored and undismissed story with raw relevance at least 0.7 and age at least six hours; or a live owned published public Telegram post at least six hours old. Each collection pass still visits at most 10 opted-in brands and 50 targets per brand; one automatic sample per target. The new setting does not turn on free collection, and switching off paid analysis does not turn off free collection. No new independent paid scan walks old samples.

## Consent and operator surface

Add two brand-scoped paid settings: **Analyze source replies automatically** on Sources, and **Analyze publication replies automatically** on Results. Both are false on install and on migration, even where free collection is already enabled. A brand manager, organization admin, or owner may change their brand's respective setting and brand daily threshold; viewers can read status only. Each setting has its own monotonically increasing revision, incremented on every update, including off/on. It applies only while the corresponding free collection setting is enabled. The UI says the organization’s Google AI key pays for each attempt and describes the bounded sample. A missing key shows a Settings link and a waiting state; enabling may be saved without a key but cannot dispatch a paid call. Never expose a key, Telegram session, reply author, message ID, or provider error text.

The two paid toggles are separate; their spend allowance is shared. Propose one brand-wide paid-reply daily threshold (default USD 1.00) and one organization-wide paid-reply daily threshold (default USD 5.00), configured by owner/admin at organization level and by manager or higher for the brand. A brand manager cannot raise the brand value above the organization value. Both thresholds are admission controls, not invoice caps. The day is the organization's configured IANA timezone calendar day; store that timezone with each admission so a later timezone edit cannot rewrite history. Reject invalid timezone, nonpositive threshold, and values beyond a small server maximum. An operator sees current-day admitted cost and any blocked reason before changing a setting.

## Sample identity and transactional handoff

Give every completed saved collection a new immutable `sample_version` UUID in the same transaction as its reply rows and check status. This version changes on a manual refresh too; timestamps alone are insufficient to distinguish two samples. For a source, it belongs to the checked story; for a publication, it belongs to the existing sample row. An analysis reads the exact ordered newest 30 rows for that version and freezes the bounded prompt input (title and reply bodies, or a digest plus a versioned immutable snapshot) before admission. It must not silently mix rows from a later refresh. The existing at-most-50 collection limit and author-free storage remain.

When an automatic collector successfully saves `available` with at least one readable reply, it may insert one paid attempt **and** enqueue its job in that same database transaction, after the collection write. The insert requires the paid and free settings to be enabled at the revisions observed for this job, a live eligible target, and the saved `sample_version`; lock the paid setting so opt-out serializes with this commit. No attempt or job is created for `no_comments`, `unavailable`, `error`, a stale collection, or a manual refresh. A queue failure rolls back the sample write, attempt, and enqueue together. A lost job may be redispatched from its existing queued attempt; it must never create a fresh attempt for that sample. The queue singleton is an optimization, not the money-safety invariant.

## At most one paid request per exact sample

Use a durable `paid_reply_analysis_attempts` row with `org_id`, `brand_id`, target kind/id, `sample_version`, prompt digest, origin (`automatic` or `manual`), both consent revisions where relevant, admission date/timezone, reserved maximum USD, status, timestamps, and a safe failure code. Enforce `UNIQUE (org_id, target_kind, target_id, sample_version)` across **both** automatic and manual entry points; the manual POST must use the same claim. Keep attempt history after a target is deleted (target ID is a plain UUID, not a cascading FK), while the saved analysis can cascade. A second request for the same version returns its existing state or result. If a paid attempt failed or timed out, retry requires collecting a new sample, and the UI explains that action. This intentional change closes the current manual admission lease expiry path that can otherwise buy a second call for the same sample.

Before contacting Gemini, atomically move `queued` to `dispatching` under a row lock and commit that claim. `dispatching` is irreversible for that sample: timeout, worker death, lease expiry, queue retry, or ambiguous provider response becomes `unknown`/`failed` for display and accounting, never a second provider call. A worker may retry pre-dispatch checks or recover a lost queued job, but may not call the provider unless it performed the single successful transition itself. Do not hold a database transaction across the network call. Set SDK retries and structured-output repair retries to zero. A provider refusal known to have happened before any request may be recorded as failed, but still does not reopen the sample. Record every physical call via the existing usage hook in its own transaction before saving a result, linked uniquely to the attempt/admission; a lost usage write increments a durable unrecorded-call marker and blocks further automatic admission. Never show a completed result when metering failed.

## Conservative admission and model bound

Serialize admission on the organization row, shared across both target kinds, managers, and worker replicas. Use a single explicit Google model ID whose current date has a known `@pubrick/ai` price; do not inherit the user-editable default model or fall back to a platform key. Proposed model: `gemini-3.7-flash`, subject to an implementation-time check that its provider and SDK accept the chosen output bound. Limit the caller to one request, the current 30 × 500 character input plus a 300-character title, and at most 1,024 total generated tokens **including any thinking tokens**. Compute a conservative maximum USD from the pinned input byte bound, the full output-token bound, and the applicable dated price rate. If the SDK/provider cannot enforce or confirm that output accounting bound, automatic analysis remains disabled until a bounded known-priced model is chosen. Any unknown model price, absent key, invalid price window, or failed ledger read refuses admission before dispatch.

At admission, count **all** organization AI ledger spend for the local day, plus every outstanding paid reply reservation (manual and automatic) at its maximum cost; compare against the organization threshold. For the brand, count all attributable AI ledger spend that day, plus outstanding reservations across source and publication analysis. New reply ledger rows must carry `brand_id`; until other legacy rows can be attributed, charge unattributed organization spend against **each** brand's admission calculation rather than treat it as zero. If either `spent + outstanding reservations + new maximum` exceeds its threshold, do not queue the attempt. A row with unknown price, a non-rejected null cost, an unrecorded call marker from analysis or generation, or an unavailable ledger query fails closed for the affected organization/day (and therefore its brands). An attempt that gets no provider dispatch releases its reservation; a dispatched ambiguous attempt retains its maximum reservation through the day unless a priced usage row settles it. The actual invoice can exceed these thresholds through provider billing differences, prior in-flight work outside this feature, and the estimate's limitations; label them **daily admission thresholds**, never hard spending caps or guaranteed maximum charges.

The existing rolling organization allowance of 10 comment-analysis admissions per hour still includes both kinds and manual calls. Its reservation is taken in the same locked admission transaction as the daily checks and paid-attempt insertion. No duplicate hourly reservation or ledger row is created on queue redelivery. Record distinct reasons such as `hourly_limit`, `brand_daily_threshold`, `org_daily_threshold`, `unknown_spend`, `unpriced_model`, and `no_key` without exposing internal provider text.

## Fences, races, and results

Before the irreversible dispatch transition, recheck organization and brand, public Telegram target and original channel/source ownership, current `sample_version`, nonempty saved rows, Google key, both enabled settings and their revisions, and the daily admission state. A disabled setting or off/on revision change revokes queued work. Serialize that last pre-dispatch check with the setting update; if opt-out commits first, there is no call. If dispatch commits first, the provider request may finish after opt-out; the UI must say that an in-flight charge cannot be recalled. A manual refresh, source dismissal/deactivation, publication/channel deletion, or brand deletion before dispatch cancels the queued attempt without spending. After dispatch, keep its ledger and attempt even if the target disappears; write an analysis result only if the same live target, ownership and sample version still match, otherwise mark it stale without rethrowing. Later samples mark prior analyses stale and never automatically trigger a second call. Do not publish, create content, or send Telegram replies from any analysis outcome.

Read-only analysis responses expose `queued`, `analyzing`, `ready`, `stale`, `no_comments`, `no_key`, `blocked` (with safe reason), `failed`, and `unknown` as appropriate, alongside sample size and analyzed time for a ready result. `unknown` means a request may have been charged and is not retried. Keep previous ready analysis visible as an **earlier sample** when a later check fails. Results are aggregate sentiment, themes, and feedback only, with the existing warning that this sample is not the whole audience. Opening either panel is read-only; its Check action polls saved state and never buys a call. The manual Analyze action remains explicit for samples without an existing attempt.

## Migration, rollout, and acceptance

Migrate first: add default-false source/publication paid configs, stable sample versions, durable attempt/admission fields and unique constraint, brand attribution on new ledger rows, indexes and database checks. Backfill existing sample versions without creating automatic attempts or jobs; do not infer consent. For any pre-migration sample with an existing manual admission, create one consumed attempt tombstone for its current version, or require a fresh collection before another manual call; old completed analyses remain readable. Deploy shared DTOs and API readers/writers, then worker queue handling, then the EN/ES/RU/PT UI strings and controls. Keep the automatic dispatcher feature flag off until all processes run the new schema and the price/output-bound preflight passes. Turn it on only for newly completed automatic collections. Update `docs/telegram-sources.md`, `docs/publication-comments.md`, `docs/automatic-telegram-comments.md`, and `docs/porting-status.md` when behavior ships.

Focused acceptance tests must prove: both toggles default off and are manager/tenant scoped; enabling paid alone performs no collection or call; successful nonempty **automatic** source and publication collections each queue once in their save transaction; empty/error/manual/old samples queue none; opt-out/off-on, stale sample, dismissal, deletion and wrong org cancel before spend; concurrent collectors/manual POSTs and lost-job redispatch keep one durable attempt and one physical call; timeout/lease expiry never redial; metering occurs before result and ledger loss blocks subsequent admission; unknown price/spend/key blocks; brand and org day boundaries, outstanding reservations, cross-kind rolling-hour limit, and concurrent brands cannot over-admit; old analysis becomes stale after refresh; all four locale screens show consent, cost warning and safe statuses without author data, automatic action, or provider secrets.

## Open decisions proposed for implementation review

1. **Threshold values:** Start with USD 1.00 per brand and USD 5.00 per organization per local day, adjustable only within server bounds. These are deliberately small admission thresholds. Confirm with product before rollout, not by silently choosing larger values.
2. **Prompt snapshot:** Prefer storing a digest and immutable bounded snapshot on the attempt so a queued worker analyzes precisely the admitted rows after any concurrent refresh. Encrypt or otherwise restrict the snapshot like saved reply bodies and delete it on the same retention schedule as samples; keep the non-sensitive attempt identity and accounting history.
3. **Provider token bound:** Verify the chosen Gemini model and SDK count reasoning within `maxOutputTokens`. If that property cannot be established by a focused provider-contract test and current documentation, use a model with an enforceable bound or leave automatic dispatch off. The UI must never call an estimated admission threshold a hard invoice cap.
