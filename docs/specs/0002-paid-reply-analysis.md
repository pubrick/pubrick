# Paid analysis after automatic Telegram reply collection

**Status:** Proposed design; no implementation or rollout implied.
**Scope:** Source stories and owned Telegram publications only.

## Decision and existing contract

The remaining verified legacy behavior is hourly Gemini analysis of replies to relevant source posts and our own published Telegram posts. The legacy worker fetched replies and analyzed them in one task, wrote a result even for empty or inaccessible discussions, and recorded an estimated spend afterward. Pubrick already has a safer split: separate, default-off hourly **free** collection for source stories and publications; saved text-only samples of at most 50 replies; and a manual, metered **Google BYOK** analysis of the newest 30 saved replies, with at most 500 characters per reply. This increment adds a second, explicit default-off consent to run that existing analysis automatically **only after a successful, nonempty automatic collection**. It does not analyze manual collections, old samples, private discussions, inaccessible posts, or empty/error checks. It does not create topics, drafts, feedback signals, replies, or publications.

Keep the current hourly collection eligibility and fairness limits: public active Telegram source, scored and undismissed story with raw relevance at least 0.7 and age at least six hours; or a live owned published public Telegram post at least six hours old. Each collection pass still visits at most 10 opted-in brands and 50 targets per brand; one automatic sample per target. The new setting does not turn on free collection, and switching off paid analysis does not turn off free collection. No new independent paid scan walks old samples.

## Consent and operator surface

Add two brand-scoped paid settings: **Analyze source replies automatically** on Sources, and **Analyze publication replies automatically** on Results. Both are false on install and on migration, even where free collection is already enabled. A brand manager, organization admin, or owner may change their brand's respective setting and brand daily threshold; viewers can read status only. Each setting has its own monotonically increasing revision, incremented on every update, including off/on. It applies only while the corresponding free collection setting is enabled. The UI says the organization’s Google AI key pays for each attempt and describes the bounded sample. A missing key shows a Settings link and a waiting state; enabling may be saved without a key but cannot dispatch a paid call. Never expose a key, Telegram session, reply author, message ID, or provider error text.

The two paid toggles are separate; their spend allowance is shared. Propose one brand-wide paid-reply daily threshold (default USD 1.00) and one organization-wide paid-reply daily threshold (default USD 5.00), configured by owner/admin at organization level and by manager or higher for the brand. A brand manager cannot raise the brand value above the organization value. Both thresholds are admission controls, not invoice caps. Add an organization paid-reply settings row with an explicit IANA `timezone` defaulting to `UTC`, organization threshold, and revision; backfill every existing organization and create the row with every new one. Only owner/admin may edit organization settings. Validate the zone against the runtime's IANA database and increment the revision on every edit. Reject invalid timezone, nonpositive threshold, and values beyond a small server maximum. An operator sees current-day admitted cost and any blocked reason before changing a setting.

The admission day is the organization's local calendar date at the database admission instant. Compute its **two UTC instants** by converting local midnight and the next local midnight in the stored zone; query `[startUtc, endUtc)`. A DST day may be 23 or 25 hours; never add 24 hours to `startUtc`. Persist local date, zone, both UTC bounds, organization revision and price-window identity on the admission. `usage_ledger.created_at` is a legacy naive UTC timestamp: interpret it as UTC with `created_at AT TIME ZONE 'UTC'` before comparing with those UTC instants. Treat an ambiguous or invalid clock conversion as a refusal, not zero spend.

## Sample identity and transactional handoff

Persist `sample_version` as the version of the **saved reply-row set**, distinct from check status, request time and check time. A successful replacement or clearing of rows changes it in the same transaction as those rows; an error, unavailable check, or pending refresh that retains the previous rows keeps the previous version and does not stale its ready analysis. If no row set has ever been saved, the version is null. For a source it belongs to the story; for a publication it belongs to the existing sample row. An analysis reads the exact newest 30 rows ordered by `published_at DESC, id DESC` for both target kinds (the source query currently lacks the ID tie-breaker) and freezes the bounded title/reply input for that version. It must not mix rows from a later refresh. The existing at-most-50 collection limit and author-free storage remain.

Use two short transactions. **Collection transaction:** save the free result independently. If the paid setting is enabled at the locked finish point, a nonempty successful automatic collection also writes one durable pending handoff keyed by `(org, kind, target, sample_version)` with both consent revisions; do not reserve money or enqueue paid work yet. No handoff is written for `no_comments`, `unavailable`, `error`, a stale collection, or a manual refresh. **Paid transaction, after the first commits:** lock the organization first, recheck the handoff, sample, target and both free/paid consent revisions, run hourly and daily admission, insert its single attempt/admission pair, and enqueue the paid job using pg-boss's transaction binding. Mark the handoff dispatched in the same transaction. If paid enqueue fails, roll back only this transaction: the free sample and pending handoff remain, and a bounded reconciler retries pending handoffs for that newly collected version. A policy refusal marks the handoff blocked with its reason and does not retry it automatically on a later day. This is recovery of explicit handoffs, not a scan over old samples. A lost job may be redispatched from its existing queued attempt; it must never create a fresh attempt. The queue singleton is an optimization, not the money-safety invariant.

All paths that combine these rows obey the global lock order in `docs/lock-order.md`: organization parent before brand, then the applicable live target chain (source → story, or adaptation → channel → content item → publication), then sample, setting/config rows and attempt/admission rows in a fixed order. Refactor both current automatic collectors, which lock config before target today, to this order; setting edits and deletes must follow it too. Never acquire an organization lock after a sample/config lock, including through an implicit foreign-key check. Queue insertion is last. No network call runs under any of these locks.

## At most one paid request per exact sample

Use a durable `paid_reply_analysis_attempts` row with `org_id`, `brand_id`, target kind/id, `sample_version`, prompt digest, origin (`automatic` or `manual`), consent revisions where relevant, admission date/timezone, reserved maximum USD, status, timestamps, and a safe failure code. Enforce `UNIQUE (org_id, target_kind, target_id, sample_version)` across **both** automatic and manual entry points; the manual POST must use the same claim. Keep attempt history after a target is deleted (target ID is a plain UUID, not a cascading FK), while the saved analysis can cascade. A second request for the same version returns its existing state or result. If a paid attempt failed or timed out, retry requires collecting a new sample, and the UI explains that action.

Keep `analysis_admissions` as the **sole owner** of the existing 10-call rolling-hour count, `usage_ledger.analysis_admission_id` foreign key, and `unrecorded_calls` marker. Each new attempt has exactly one admission, linked by a unique non-null FK for new rows; insert both in one transaction after the attempt uniqueness check. The attempt owns sample identity, dispatch state, snapshot and daily reservation. Do not count both tables or mint a second admission on redelivery. Retire `admitAnalysis`'s current behavior of completing an expired two-minute lease and inserting a fresh admission for the same target/sample. Lease expiry becomes a recovery signal: queued work may be redelivered with the same pair; `dispatching` work is swept to `unknown` without another call. Cancel an older queued admission before admitting a replacement sample for the same target; a dispatching older sample holds the target until it settles, without a second provider call. Pre-migration admissions and both legacy source/publication ledger steps remain in the rolling-hour count without double-counting linked rows.

Before contacting Gemini, atomically move `queued` to `dispatching` under the parent-first locks and commit that claim. `dispatching` is irreversible for that sample: timeout, worker death, lease expiry, queue retry, or ambiguous provider response becomes `unknown`/`failed` for display and accounting, never a second provider call. A worker may retry pre-dispatch checks or recover a lost queued job, but may not call the provider unless it performed the single successful transition itself. Do not hold a database transaction across the network call. Set SDK retries and structured-output repair retries to zero. A provider refusal known to have happened before any request may be recorded as failed, but still does not reopen the sample.

The usage hook writes each **observed** physical call to `usage_ledger` in its own transaction before saving a result, linked to the existing admission. A process can die before the hook runs, and a database outage can prevent both the ledger insert and an `unrecorded_calls` update; do not promise that every physical call or loss marker always persists. The committed `dispatching` state and maximum reservation are the durable fallback. A sweeper turns a stale `dispatching` attempt without a priced usage row into `unknown`, retains its maximum reservation, and blocks new automatic admissions for that organization until the accounting gap is reconciled. If a hook write fails but the database recovers, increment `unrecorded_calls`; if this marker also fails, the sweeper still detects the gap. Never save or display a completed result when metering failed or the attempt lost its fence.

## Conservative admission and model bound

Serialize admission on the organization row, shared across both target kinds, managers, and worker replicas. Use a single explicit Google model ID whose current date has a known `@pubrick/ai` price; do not inherit the user-editable default model or fall back to a platform key. Proposed model: `gemini-3.7-flash`. Change the shared `GenerateStructuredArgs` API to accept `maxOutputTokens` and forward it to **every** underlying `generateText` call; `CommentAnalysisCaller` must set it to 1,024 and disable SDK and schema retries. The current shared API has no output cap. Google's [thinking guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/thinking) says this is an infrastructure cutoff including thought tokens, and its [request reference](https://ai.google.dev/api/generate-content) defines `maxOutputTokens`. A provider-contract test must inspect the actual Google request body and metering to prove the bound is forwarded and includes reasoning tokens. Until that passes, paid automatic dispatch stays off.

Bound the **entire serialized UTF-8 provider request body**, not just 30 × 500 reply characters and a 300-character title: count fixed instructions, structured-output schema, JSON framing, and every dynamic field exactly as sent. Reject a request exceeding 128 KiB before admission; reserve for an input-token ceiling derived from the full byte count **plus verified provider framing/token overhead**, never from comment characters alone. The provider-contract test must prove or conservatively bound that translation; otherwise dispatch stays off. Keep the saved sample and show a safe `request_too_large` blocked reason. Compute the maximum USD from that input ceiling and 1,024 output/thought tokens at the applicable dated price. Recheck model ID, current price window, serialized payload and cap at dispatch. Google's [pricing table](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) changes Gemini 3.7 Flash input/output rates on 2027-01-01; a queued attempt crossing that boundary cannot spend against yesterday's reservation. Unknown model price, absent key, invalid price window, failed serialization, or failed ledger read refuses dispatch.

At admission, count **all** organization AI ledger spend in the stored UTC day window, plus every outstanding paid reply reservation (manual and automatic) at its maximum cost; compare against the organization threshold. For the brand, count all attributable AI ledger spend in that window, plus outstanding reservations across source and publication analysis. New reply ledger rows must carry `brand_id`; until other legacy rows can be attributed, charge unattributed organization spend against **each** brand's admission calculation rather than treat it as zero. If either `spent + outstanding reservations + new maximum` exceeds its threshold, do not queue the attempt. A row with unknown price, a non-rejected null cost, an unrecorded call marker from analysis or generation, or an unavailable ledger query fails closed for the affected organization/day (and therefore its brands). An attempt that gets no provider dispatch releases its reservation; a dispatched ambiguous attempt retains its maximum reservation and accounting block until reconciled. The actual invoice can exceed these thresholds through provider billing differences, prior in-flight work outside this feature, and the estimate's limitations; label them **daily admission thresholds**, never hard spending caps or guaranteed maximum charges.

The existing rolling organization allowance of 10 comment-analysis admissions per hour still includes both kinds and manual calls. Its reservation is taken in the same locked admission transaction as the daily checks and paid-attempt insertion. No duplicate hourly reservation or ledger row is created on queue redelivery. Record distinct reasons such as `hourly_limit`, `brand_daily_threshold`, `org_daily_threshold`, `unknown_spend`, `unpriced_model`, and `no_key` without exposing internal provider text.

## Fences, races, and results

Before the irreversible dispatch transition, recheck organization and brand, public Telegram target and original channel/source ownership, current `sample_version`, nonempty saved rows, Google key, both enabled settings and their revisions, and the daily admission state. The worker compares the current database time with the attempt's stored UTC day window, organization timezone/settings revision, and price-window identity. If midnight, a timezone edit, or a price change moved any of those boundaries, **cancel** the queued attempt and release its reservation; never spend under an old day's or old price's allowance. A new automatic attempt for that same sample is not minted. A disabled setting or off/on revision change also revokes queued work. Serialize that last pre-dispatch check with the setting update; if opt-out commits first, there is no call. If dispatch commits first, the provider request may finish after opt-out; the UI must say that an in-flight charge cannot be recalled. A manual refresh that replaces rows, source dismissal/deactivation, publication/channel deletion, or brand deletion before dispatch cancels the queued attempt without spending. A failed refresh retaining rows does not cancel it merely because check time/status changed. After dispatch, keep its ledger and attempt even if the target disappears; write an analysis result only if the same live target, ownership and sample version still match, otherwise mark it stale without rethrowing. A later replacement sample can create its own single automatic attempt when consent and admission still allow it; it never reopens or repeats the prior sample's attempt. Do not publish, create content, or send Telegram replies from any analysis outcome.

Read-only analysis responses expose `queued`, `analyzing`, `ready`, `stale`, `no_comments`, `no_key`, `blocked` (with safe reason), `failed`, and `unknown` as appropriate, alongside sample size and analyzed time for a ready result. `unknown` means a request may have been charged and is not retried. Extend the shared DTO to a compound response, for example `{ current: { status, sampleVersion, reason? }, earlierAnalysis?: { sampleVersion, result, sampleSize, analyzedAt } }`, so a newer failed/empty check can describe its current state **and** retain the earlier ready aggregate with an explicit “Earlier sample” label. When a failed refresh retained the same reply-row version, that analysis is still current, though the collection check has failed; report both facts rather than falsely calling the analysis stale. Results are aggregate sentiment, themes, and feedback only, with the existing warning that this sample is not the whole audience. Opening either panel is read-only; its Check action polls saved state and never buys a call. The manual Analyze action remains explicit for samples without an existing attempt.

Freeze the bounded serialized prompt snapshot on the attempt, encrypted using the existing application encryption key; the prompt digest alone is insufficient to reconstruct the admitted request after rows change. Keep the snapshot while queued or dispatching. On any terminal state, purge ciphertext after 30 days, and purge it immediately on target/brand deletion once an in-flight call has ended; an organization deletion follows its normal tenant-erasure cascade. The attempt's non-content tombstone, digest, unique sample identity, admission link, reservation and ledger attribution remain for the organization's accounting history. A purge worker must be idempotent and must not reopen or delete the unique claim. Access to snapshot plaintext is server-side only and restricted to the claiming worker; API DTOs never return it.

## Migration, rollout, and acceptance

Migrate first: add the organization timezone/threshold settings with default UTC and USD 5.00, default-false source/publication paid configs, stable reply-row sample versions, durable handoff and attempt rows, 1:1 admission link and unique constraint, brand attribution on new ledger rows, indexes and database checks. Backfill existing reply-row sets with versions without creating automatic handoffs or jobs; do not infer consent. For any pre-migration sample with an existing manual admission, create one consumed attempt tombstone for its current version, or require a fresh row-replacing collection before another manual call; old completed analyses remain readable. Deploy the shared `generateStructured` cap and DTOs, then API readers/writers and corrected lock order, then worker queue handling, then the EN/ES/RU/PT UI strings and controls. Keep the automatic dispatcher feature flag off until all processes run the new schema and the actual provider-request/output-bound contract test passes. Turn it on only for newly completed automatic collections. Update `docs/lock-order.md`, `docs/telegram-sources.md`, `docs/publication-comments.md`, `docs/automatic-telegram-comments.md`, and `docs/porting-status.md` when behavior ships.

Focused acceptance tests must prove: both toggles default off and are manager/tenant scoped; organization timezone defaults UTC and only owner/admin can edit it; enabling paid alone performs no collection or call; successful nonempty **automatic** source and publication collections persist a free sample and handoff, then queue once in the separate paid transaction; a failed paid enqueue leaves the free sample and recoverable handoff intact; empty/error/manual/old samples queue none; opt-out/off-on, replaced sample, dismissal, deletion and wrong org cancel before spend; failed/unavailable refresh retaining reply rows keeps `sample_version` and ready analysis; source rows tied at the 30th newest timestamp select deterministically by ID; concurrent collectors/manual POSTs and lost-job redispatch keep one attempt, one admission and one physical call; timeout/lease expiry never redial, and worker/DB loss after dispatch remains unknown with its reservation; metering precedes result and ledger loss blocks subsequent admission; unknown price/spend/key blocks; 23/25-hour DST days, naive-UTC ledger conversion, midnight/timezone edit and 2027 price-boundary cancellation, outstanding reservations, cross-kind rolling-hour limit, and concurrent brands cannot over-admit; a provider-body test captures the full UTF-8 request and `maxOutputTokens`, including reasoning in the output limit; parent-first interleavings of collection, paid admission, opt-out and deletion have no deadlock; snapshot purge retains the unique accounting tombstone; compound DTO and all four locale screens show the current check plus earlier analysis safely, without author data, automatic content action or provider secrets.

## Open decisions proposed for implementation review

1. **Threshold values:** Start with USD 1.00 per brand and USD 5.00 per organization per local day, adjustable only within server bounds. These are deliberately small admission thresholds. Confirm with product before rollout, not by silently choosing larger values.
2. **Provider adapter:** The request-body and output-cap tests may require a narrow preparation seam around the Google SDK. Prefer a maintained SDK hook; otherwise add the smallest private seam needed to inspect the exact outbound request. If the provider changes serialization, the admission preflight must fail closed until the bound is revalidated.
